Executive summary

Intrinsic readiness is observed (survey-based). Equilibrium readiness is modelled (after network propagation). ARR is the folksonomy-based ranking (centrality in the respondent–factor graph), not readiness.

Five readiness dimensions

Dimension value = arithmetic mean of its factors' readiness. Rank is by equilibrium readiness (1 = highest).

Immediate attention — Model-based diagnostic signal

Factors with the lowest equilibrium readiness. This is a model-based diagnostic signal derived from association structure, not a causal recommendation.

Amplification

Standardised one-factor experiments: each factor's intrinsic anchor is raised by the same relative percentage, one factor at a time, with W fixed, and the equilibrium is recomputed.

Standardised intervention (%)
Dimension
Amplification index = Σj≠i max(0, ΔR*j) ÷ Δai. This is a normalised ratio: positive spillover per unit of direct anchor improvement. Absolute positive spillover = Σj≠i max(0, ΔR*j) in percentage points. It scales with the size of the direct change, which under a relative intervention is larger for factors with higher intrinsic readiness. The two measures can rank factors differently.

Resilience: shock and recovery

For each factor independently, the system starts at the baseline equilibrium R*. The factor's intrinsic anchor is cut by the shock magnitude for the shock duration, then restored, and propagation continues to the horizon. Overall AIR(t) is tracked throughout.

Alternative network constructions (τ and α at current values)

The same intrinsic anchors are propagated through networks built in different ways. Jaccard and graded constructions keep the joint-endorsement structure. Lift and correlation networks remove the effect of how often a factor is endorsed. ρ = Spearman correlation with the baseline equilibrium configuration.

Respondent bootstrap (sampling uncertainty)

Respondents are resampled with replacement. Each resample re-estimates a, W, ARR and R* at the current τ, α and d. The seeded generator (mulberry32) makes the results exactly reproducible. Intervals are percentile 95% intervals, and ranks are 1 = highest.

Measurement diagnostics

Internal consistency (Cronbach's α), Harman-type single-factor test (share of the first principal component of the item correlation matrix), and within-group agreement rwg against a uniform null distribution (σ²EU = 2 for a 5-point scale). The multi-item index rwg(j) is computed across all 18 items.
